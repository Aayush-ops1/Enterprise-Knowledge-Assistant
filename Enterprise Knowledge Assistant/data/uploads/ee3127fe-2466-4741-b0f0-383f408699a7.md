# Incident Management Policy

## 1. Severity Levels

### 1.1 Severity Definitions
- SEV1: Production service is down or customer data is at risk. Examples include total platform outage or a data loss event.
- SEV2: A major feature is degraded or unavailable, but the platform remains usable. Examples include a slow search module or a failing payment webhook.
- SEV3: Minor issues that do not affect customers, such as a cosmetic defect in the admin console.

### 1.2 Examples and Owner
SEV1 incidents are led by the on-call engineer with the support of the engineering manager. SEV2 incidents are handled by the owning team during business hours and by the on-call engineer outside them. SEV3 incidents are triaged into the normal backlog.

## 2. On-Call and Response SLAs

### 2.1 On-Call Rotation
The on-call rotation is weekly, with one primary and one secondary engineer per team. The on-call engineer carries the incident phone and must acknowledge pages within the response SLA.

### 2.2 Response and Update SLAs
- SEV1: acknowledge within 15 minutes, provide status updates every 30 minutes.
- SEV2: acknowledge within 30 minutes, provide status updates every 60 minutes.
- SEV3: acknowledged next business day, no ongoing updates required.

## 3. Escalation Path

### 3.1 Escalation Ladder
Escalation follows this ladder: on-call engineer (Level 1), team lead (Level 2), engineering manager (Level 3), and the CTO (Level 4). A level is escalated if the incident is not resolved or contained within 30 minutes for SEV1, and 60 minutes for SEV2.

### 3.2 Communication
All incident communication happens in the #incidents Slack channel. Customer-facing status is posted to the public status page for SEV1 incidents. Internal announcements are sent for SEV2 incidents lasting over 60 minutes.

## 4. Incident Handling

### 4.1 Containment First
The first priority is containment, not diagnosis. The on-call engineer may roll back a deployment, disable a feature flag, or scale up capacity to stop the impact, and may do so without prior approval.

### 4.2 Status Page
The status page must be updated within 15 minutes of an SEV1 declaration and every 30 minutes thereafter, with plain-language descriptions of the impact and expected recovery.

## 5. Postmortems

### 5.1 Postmortem Timeline
A blameless postmortem document must be published within 5 business days for every SEV1 and any SEV2 that lasted over 60 minutes.

### 5.2 Postmortem Content and Actions
The postmortem covers the timeline, root cause, impact, and a set of action items to prevent recurrence. Action items are tracked in the incident tracker, and follow-up ownership is assigned to a named owner with a due date. The blameless principle means the postmortem focuses on system and process failures, not individual blame.
